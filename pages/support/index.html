Support

FlashReader is an Android app for reading your own books and texts at your own pace — including speed reading with adjustable words per minute.

Import files into a local library, continue where you left off, or paste text for a quick session. Books and documents stay on your device; you do not create an account to use the app.

Contact

For help with FlashReader, email [SUPPORT EMAIL].

We read support mail as it arrives. How quickly we can reply depends on volume. Privacy questions can also go to the address listed in the Privacy Policy.

What to include

A complete report is easier to investigate. Please include:

  • App version — shown at the bottom of Settings (for example “Version 1.0.0”)
  • Device — manufacturer and model (for example Pixel 8 or Samsung Galaxy S24)
  • Android version — from your device Settings → About phone
  • What happened — what you expected and what you saw instead, including any error text
  • Steps to reproduce — the actions that lead to the problem, in order

Do not send books, personal documents, or screenshots that contain private text unless we specifically ask for them and you are willing to share that material.

Frequently asked questions

Which formats does FlashReader support?

You can import:

  • EPUB
  • FB2 and FB2.ZIP
  • TXT
  • Markdown
  • HTML

You can also write or paste text in the app, and load a YouTube transcript from a video URL when captions are available.

Where are my books stored?

Imported books, texts you write or paste, covers, bookmarks, reading position, settings, and loaded transcripts are stored in the app’s storage on your device. FlashReader does not require an account.

Your library content is not sent to Firebase Analytics or Google AdMob. If you use the YouTube transcript feature, the video URL may be sent to a network service so the app can request captions. See the Privacy Policy for how content and technical data are treated separately.

What happens if I uninstall the app?

Uninstalling FlashReader deletes the app’s local storage on that device, including your library, bookmarks, and settings — except copies that may remain in an operating-system or cloud backup you created.

Uninstalling does not guarantee that Google will delete information already processed by Firebase Analytics, AdMob, UMP, or Google Play Billing. Purchases stay associated with your Google account under Google Play’s rules.

How do I change my advertising consent?

In regions where Google requires a consent message (including the EEA, UK, and Switzerland), FlashReader uses Google’s User Messaging Platform. You can change your choices later from Manage Privacy in FlashReader Settings when that option is shown.

Consent choices affect whether analytics and ads may run. They do not delete your local library.

How do I remove ads?

FlashReader may show advertisements through Google AdMob. You may be able to purchase an option that removes ads through Google Play Billing. Google processes the payment; we do not collect your full payment card details.

Removing ads hides advertisements. It does not remove Firebase Analytics or other technical processing described in the Privacy Policy, except where those services are tied to the same consent or purchase rules.

How do I restore a purchase?

A purchase is tied to the Google account that paid for it. On a device signed into the same Google account, ask FlashReader to restore the purchase so the app can read the purchase status from Google Play and unlock the feature again.

If the feature does not unlock, email [SUPPORT EMAIL] with your Google Play order details so we can help you restore the purchase or point you to Google Play support. Refunds follow Google Play’s policies.

How do YouTube transcripts work?

You submit a YouTube video URL. That URL may be transmitted to a network service so FlashReader can request available captions or transcript data. If a transcript is returned, the app stores it locally like other texts.

  • A transcript may be incomplete, inaccurate, delayed, or unavailable. FlashReader does not control YouTube’s caption data.
  • You are responsible for choosing a URL you are allowed to request and for complying with YouTube’s terms.
  • The returned text is for reading in the app. It is not an official or complete record of the video.
